🎯 The Four Pillars of GDMT for HFrEF

All four drug classes reduce mortality. Together they reduce all-cause mortality by an estimated 73% vs no treatment.2,3

💡 Key Principle: Start all four classes early (within 4–6 weeks), then uptitrate to target by 3 months. Low-dose quadruple therapy is superior to high-dose monotherapy.4

🔴 HFrEF (LVEF ≤40%) — Quadruple Therapy

All four classes should be started, ideally within 4–6 weeks of diagnosis, then uptitrated to target (or maximally tolerated) doses within 3 months.4

💊 The Four Pillars — Detailed

1. RAAS Inhibition — ARNI Preferred

Sacubitril/valsartan (ARNI) is preferred over ACE inhibitors and ARBs in chronic HFrEF with NYHA class II–III symptoms. In PARADIGM-HF it reduced all-cause mortality by a 2.8% absolute margin vs enalapril.1,3
  • ARNI: Sacubitril/valsartan — start 24/26 mg BID, target 97/103 mg BID
  • ACEi: If ARNI not feasible — e.g., lisinopril, enalapril
  • ARB: Third-line for ACE/ARNI intolerance
  • ⚠️ Washout: Allow 36-hour washout from ACE inhibitor before starting ARNI to avoid angioedema

2. Beta-Blocker

Use a cardioprotective agent only: carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, or bisoprolol. These reduce death and hospitalization.3

⚠️ Important: Defer beta-blocker initiation until HF is compensated/euvolemic. Do not start during acute decompensation.

3. Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonist (MRA)

Spironolactone or eplerenone. Avoid if eGFR ≤30 mL/min, and discontinue if potassium cannot be kept <5.5 mEq/L.3

4. SGLT2 Inhibitor

Dapagliflozin or empagliflozin, regardless of diabetes status (Class 1, LOE A). Reduces cardiovascular death and HF hospitalization.5,1

🟠 HFmrEF (LVEF 41–49%)

Use diuretics for fluid overload, and an SGLT2 inhibitor (Class 2a). Post-hoc analyses support benefit from all four GDMT classes.3

📋 Management Approach

SGLT2i Class 2a Best-supported therapy; start early
Diuretics Class 1 For congestion/fluid overload
Beta-blockers Consider (post-hoc) In sinus rhythm
ARNI/ACEi/ARB Consider (post-hoc) Greater benefit at lower EF end
MRA Consider (post-hoc) Monitor K⁺ and renal function
💡 Clinical Pearl: HFmrEF often behaves more like HFrEF than HFpEF. Post-hoc analyses of major trials (PARAGON-HF, TOPCAT, etc.) suggest benefit from neurohormonal blockade, especially when EF is toward 41–45%.

🟢 HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%) — 2026 ACC Consensus Update

Historically the hardest group to treat, but the evidence base has grown substantially. The 2026 ACC Expert Consensus Decision Pathway reframes HFpEF as a multisystem syndrome driven by visceral adiposity, inflammatory adipokines, and metabolic dysfunction.11

🔵 HF with Improved EF (HFimpEF)

Definition: Previous LVEF ≤40% and follow-up measurement of LVEF >40%.1

📋 Management Principle

Continue the GDMT originally indicated for HFrEF even after EF recovers (Class 1, LOE B-R). Withdrawal risks relapse.1

The TRED-HF trial demonstrated a 44% rate of relapse of dilated cardiomyopathy within 6 months of discontinuing GDMT in patients with recovered DCM. Therefore, GDMT should be continued in patients with HFimpEF, including those who are asymptomatic.8

⚡ Acute Heart Failure — STRONG-HF Strategy

For acutely hospitalized patients, the STRONG-HF strategy of rapid uptitration to target doses within ~2 weeks of discharge with close follow-up reduced 180-day readmission or death by 34%.3

📋 STRONG-HF Protocol

TimingAction
2 days before discharge Prescribe at least half optimal doses of beta-blocker, ACEi/ARB/ARNI, and MRA
2 weeks post-discharge Uptitrate to full optimal doses of all three classes
Follow-up visits 1, 2, 3, and 6 weeks post-discharge with lab monitoring (renal function, K⁺, NT-proBNP)
💡 Key Insight: Rapid uptitration before and early after discharge is feasible, safe, and improves outcomes regardless of HF etiology (ischemic vs nonischemic).13

🏥 In-Hospital Initiation Principles

  • Initiate/uptitrate GDMT within 3 days of admission — this is the most important intervention.5
  • Prioritize starting multiple GDMT at low doses over optimizing a few.5
  • Uptitrate to at least 50% of target doses during hospitalization.5
  • SGLT2i and MRA should be initiated early during hospitalization.5
  • Beta-blocker: start only when euvolemic/compensated.

💧 Decongestion Strategy

  • Loop diuretics — first-line, personalized starting dose.
  • Escalate promptly if insufficient decongestive response.
  • Consider combination diuretic therapy (loop + thiazide) for resistance.
  • Monitor urine output, weight, renal function, and electrolytes daily.

🔧 Device Therapy — ICD & CRT

Reassess ventricular function 3–6 months after target doses are reached to determine need for an ICD (sudden death prevention) and/or CRT.4

⚡ ICD Indications

CriteriaDetails
Primary prevention LVEF ≤35%, NYHA class II–III symptoms, on ≥3 months of optimal GDMT, good life expectancy
Secondary prevention Survivor of cardiac arrest or hemodynamically unstable VT

💡 Key Point: CRT benefits are greatest with LBBB morphology and wider QRS. Many CRT devices include ICD capability (CRT-D).

🏃 Non-Pharmacologic & Holistic Management

Applies to all EF categories. These interventions are important but often underutilized.16

🍎 Diet & Nutrition

  • Sodium: AHA/ACC suggests <2,300 mg/day (evidence limited); individualize targets.3
  • Dietary patterns: Mediterranean, DASH-style, or plant-based diets are reasonable.16
  • Fluid: Individualized targets; routine strict fluid restriction not universally required.
  • Dietitian referral: Emphasized as valuable support.16

🏋️ Exercise & Cardiac Rehabilitation

Exercise training / cardiac rehabilitation improves functional status and quality of life across all stages.3,16
  • Improves exercise tolerance and lowers hospitalization rates.
  • For HFrEF, reduces mortality.16
  • Despite benefits, cardiac rehabilitation remains underutilized.16
